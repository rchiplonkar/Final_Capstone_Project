From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Term used by US law enforcement agency

The United States Secret Service uses code names for U.S. presidents, first ladies, and other prominent persons and locations.[1] The use of such names was originally for security purposes and dates to a time when sensitive electronic communications were not routinely encrypted; today, the names simply serve for purposes of brevity, clarity, and tradition.[2][3] The Secret Service does not choose these names, however. The White House Communications Agency maintains a list that candidates choose from, often choosing ones that resonate with them personally.[4][5]

According to an established protocol, good codewords are unambiguous words that can be easily pronounced and readily understood by those who transmit and receive voice messages by radio or telephone regardless of their native language. Traditionally, all family members' code names start with the same letter.[4]

The code names change over time for security purposes, but are often publicly known. For security, code names are generally picked from a list of such 'good' words, but avoiding the use of common words which could likely be intended to mean their normal definitions.

Locations, objects, places and parts of Secret Service

[edit]

U.S. Secret Service codenames are not only given to people; they are often given to places, locations and even objects, such as aircraft like Air Force One, and vehicles such as the Presidential State Car.

  • Joint Base Andrews, in Prince George's County, Maryland – Acrobat or Andy[94]
  • The Presidential Motorcade – Bamboo[94]
  • The Harry S Truman Building (Department of State headquarters) – Birds-eye[94]
  • Camp David, presidential retreat in Catoctin Mountain Park, in Frederick County, Maryland – Cactus[94] or Buckeye
  • The Vice President's office – Cobweb[94]
  • The Vice President's staff – Pacemaker[94]
  • The Waldorf-Astoria Hotel, New York City – Roadhouse[94]
  • Air Force One – Angel or Cowpuncher[94]
  • The U.S. Presidential State Car – Stagecoach[40]
    • Follow-up car – Halfback[40]
  • The White House – Castle (Crown referring to the Executive Mansion, the central representative and office spaces of the White House)[95]
  • The Capitol – Punch bowl[96]
  • The White House Situation Room – Cement Mixer[96]
  • Eisenhower Executive Office Building (part of the White House Complex) – Central[97]
  • Reagan National Airport – Curbside[96]
  • The temporary residence of the President – Charcoal or Base[97]
  • The Pentagon – Calico[97]
  • White House garage – Carpet[97]
  • J. Edgar Hoover Building (FBI Headquarters) – Cork[97]
  • Lyndon Baines Johnson's ranch – Volcano[97]
  • Secret Service Counterassault Team – Hawkeye[98]
  • Secret Service Counter-sniper Team – Hercules[98]
